# Octech — Making consumer engagement feel tangible

**Project:** Octech website · **Role:** Senior UI/UX Designer · **Live site:** [octech.in](https://octech.in/)

> A complete digital experience for a consumer engagement technology company — bringing its capabilities, products, industries and campaign work into one coherent story.

---

## 01 — Overview

Octech builds the systems behind consumer promotions, loyalty programmes, branded games and AI-driven experiences for enterprise brands across FMCG, Alcobev, Pharma, Retail and Banking. It calls itself neither an agency nor a software licence business, but an engagement systems partner: strategy, engineering, creative and compliance in one team.

I designed the Octech website from the ground up: its structure, visual language, components, page templates and interaction layer, working alongside the engineering team who built it.

- **Project** — Octech corporate website
- **My role** — Senior UI/UX Designer
- **Scope** — UX strategy, information architecture, UI design, design system, interaction and motion design, visual direction
- **Platform** — Responsive website, desktop through mobile
- **Key areas of work** — Interactive hero, navigation and IA, product storytelling, industries, the case-study system, and motion and micro-interactions

> The goal wasn't a prettier brochure. It was to make a company that sells *systems* feel as tangible as the campaigns those systems run.

## 02 — The Challenge

Octech's offer is wide. On the surface there are consumer and trade promotions, brand activations, loyalty programmes, gamification, AI experiences and reward fulfilment. Underneath sit the platforms that run them (Playverra and PromoGenie), an intelligence layer covering analytics, fraud detection, personalisation and agentic integrations, a family of AI modules, and security and compliance commitments like ISO 27001:2022 and the DPDP Act, 2023.

Each of those is a reasonable thing to explain. Together they risk reading as a list.

The challenge was to make that breadth feel **clear, cohesive and easy to follow**: to let an enterprise brand manager understand in a few minutes what Octech does, what it's built on and whether it fits their category, while giving the company a stronger, more confident presence than a typical services site.

There was a tone problem too. Octech makes playful things: cricket games, voice-controlled runners, Diwali spin-the-wheels. The site had to carry that energy without undercutting the rigour enterprise buyers look for: fraud controls, auditability, data protection, uptime.

> Easy to experience, hard to explain. That gap is what the website had to close.

## 03 — Reframing Octech

Rather than presenting Octech as a list of services, I framed it as a **consumer engagement technology company**: one where campaigns are the visible layer of a system that keeps running after launch.

That reframe shaped several decisions:

- **Engagement as technology.** The site leads with what people *do*: play, scan, claim, share. Then it shows the technology that makes that reliable at scale. The homepage headline says it directly: *Consumer Engagement, Engineered for FMCG, Alcobev & Pharma.*
- **AI as experience, not buzzword.** AI shows up through what it produces and decides: personalised rewards, AI selfies, generated content, plain-language campaign insight.
- **Products as systems.** Each product is presented as a part of one ecosystem, with a clear job, rather than as a standalone feature. PromoGenie runs promotions, Playverra runs the programmes, AnalyticsGenie reads the results, SmartClaim validates claims.
- **A clear progression.** *Capabilities → Products → Industries → Case Studies*: what Octech can do, what it's built on, where it applies, and proof that it works.
- **Technology-led, but human.** A precise, restrained interface system, filled with campaign imagery that's colourful, cultural and very much made for people.

> We wanted Octech to feel less like a vendor and more like the engine behind experiences people actually enjoy.

## 04 — Information Architecture

The structure follows the questions a buyer asks, in the order they ask them:

```
What does Octech do?   →  Home: services, method, proof, platform, industries, FAQ
What can you run?      →  Solutions: promotions, activations, loyalty
What is it built on?   →  Products: PromoGenie, Playverra, AnalyticsGenie …
How do we know?        →  Intelligence: analytics, fraud, personalisation, agents
Does it fit my brand?  →  Industries: FMCG, Retail, Alcobev, Pharma, Banking
Has it worked?         →  Case Studies: filterable by mechanic
Can we trust you?      →  Why Octech: approach, integrations, MACH-ready, security
How do you think?      →  Blogs
```

Three decisions do most of the work:

**The homepage is the map.** Services, method, numbers, capabilities, platform, industries and FAQ appear in the order a first-time visitor needs them, and each block is the doorway to a deeper page. Someone who only reads the homepage still leaves with the whole picture.

**Every page points sideways.** Product, intelligence and Why Octech pages end in a "Related" row of labelled cards (*Product*, *System*, *Trust*, *Company*). A visitor reading about fraud detection is one step from the product that uses it and the compliance page that governs it. The ecosystem is navigable because the pages know about each other.

**Templates over one-offs.** Product pages share one structure: *The Problem → How It Works*, in numbered steps. Case studies share another. Twenty-plus product and case-study pages read consistently, and new ones slot in without new layout work.

> Navigation tells you where things are. The cross-links tell you how they connect.

## 05 — Visual Direction

**Typography.** One family, Urbanist, throughout. Its geometric, friendly shapes suit a company that makes games, while large, tightly set headlines give it authority. Small uppercase, letter-spaced labels, each led by a red dot ("• How we deliver", "• Our impact"), mark every section, a quiet rhythm that runs through the whole site.

**Octech Red.** A single strong red (`#F50000`), used sparingly: the logo mark, primary actions, the method timeline, highlighted figures, the floating "Chat with us" button. Keeping red rare keeps it meaningful. When something is red, it's either the brand or the thing to do next.

**Deep navy and neutrals.** A near-white base and a deep navy ink carry most of the interface. Dark navy is reserved for moments that need weight, like the case-study headers, the intelligence statements and the navigation pill.

**Accent usage.** Products get a little room of their own. Each product hero highlights one key phrase with a marker-style colour, and some products carry their own accent for actions, such as indigo on AnalyticsGenie and XQR, and purple on Playverra. They feel like distinct products while clearly belonging to Octech.

**Rounded cards and containers.** Services, case studies and industries sit in large, softly rounded containers that frame each piece of work and make the grid easy to scan.

**Spacing and hierarchy.** Generous space around a small number of big statements per section. Headline, supporting line, then detail: the hierarchy does the explaining before the copy has to.

**Imagery.** Two registers, used deliberately:
- *Product and editorial.* Real interfaces in real frames: dashboards in browsers, claim flows on tablets, reward screens on phones.
- *Human campaign imagery.* A shopper in a store aisle for ShelfVision, a chemist at the counter for Pharma, festive and sporting worlds for campaigns.

**3D.** A rendered smartphone anchors the hero. It's the one object that connects every Octech experience: almost everything the company builds is played, scanned or claimed on a phone.

> Red for the brand, navy for weight, and colour from the work itself, so the campaigns are always the loudest thing on the page.

## 06 — The Hero Experience

The hero is built around two words that never move: **We make**.

What follows them is typed, held, erased and replaced, cycling through five states:

> **We make** brands playable. · loyalty rewarding. · campaigns engaging. · data actionable. · AI personalised.

Each ending maps to one part of Octech: **gamification, loyalty, promotions, analytics and AI**. The headline stops being a tagline and becomes a guided tour of the whole company, in about 20 seconds, without a single extra click.

Around the words, the rest of the hero changes with each state:

- **A 3D smartphone** holds real campaign screens: a cricket league scoreboard, a Diwali "Spin it" wheel, a character-led game.
- **Screen transitions** swap the campaign on the phone as the story moves on.
- **The environment** behind it changes to that campaign's world, from a floodlit cricket stadium at sunset to a deep festive purple.
- **A thumbnail row** under the headline shows every campaign in the set and highlights the one on screen.
- **A time-aware greeting** runs above the headline ("Good afternoon. Let's build something people remember?"), a small signal that the site is live and responding.

I paced the typing so it reads comfortably rather than showing off. Each phrase types in quickly, holds for about two and a half seconds, clears and hands over. The words, the phone and the world behind them move as one sequence, so the eye always has one clear thing to follow.

> Instead of *describing* engagement, the hero performs it. The words say what Octech makes, and the phone and the world around it show it being made.

On mobile the same idea reorganises around the phone. The headline stacks above it, the phone becomes the centrepiece, and previous and next controls let visitors move through campaigns themselves.

## 07 — Making Complex Products Understandable

Octech's products solve problems most visitors can't see: fraud, reconciliation, attribution, shelf compliance. Every product page follows the same approach: **name the problem in plain words, then show the product doing its job.** A shared tab bar runs across all of them, so visitors can move between products like chapters.

| Product | What it does | How the page makes it visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PromoGenie | A no-code platform to create, manage and scale promotional journeys | Leads with the outcome, *"Turn promotions into participation"*, then walks the journey in numbered steps, starting with the campaign set-up and how consumers take part |
| AnalyticsGenie | One live dashboard for media spend and promotion performance | Highlights the shift in thinking, *"Know your cost per **redemption**, not just your cost per click"*, over a real dashboard in a laptop frame |
| AcademyGenie | Engagement mechanics applied to learning, so people finish courses | Shows a real learning platform in a browser, framed as *"Turn education into a digital journey"* |
| Playverra | The platform that runs campaigns, games, loyalty and rewards, configured per brand | A phone showing a live "Claim rewards" screen: the product as the loop a shopper sees, *"thirty seconds of play, one more order"* |
| GenStudio AI | Consumer co-creation (AI selfies, anthems, pack designs, squad reels) with moderation and rewards built in | A strip of what consumers actually make with it, under *"Turn passive consumers into active participants"* |
| SmartClaim AI | Automates the path from a submitted claim to an approved reward, with fraud checks at each step | A claim flow on a tablet in a real setting, with the validation path laid out as steps |
| ShelfVision AI | Reads a retail shelf with computer vision to surface stock, placement and compliance issues | A shopper in a real store aisle: the problem is shown where it happens, before the technology |
| XQR | Makes printed QR codes measurable and editable after they're printed | *"Understand every scan. Optimise every campaign."* The code is treated as infrastructure, not artwork |

> Name the problem in the customer's words. Then show the product doing the job, in a frame they recognise.

## 08 — Capabilities & Engagement Mechanics

Octech's capabilities are presented at two levels.

**Services, as big clear promises.** On the homepage, Consumer Promotions, Loyalty Programmes, Gamification and AI Experiences each get a large card with a one-line promise and a real campaign visual. The promises are written around outcomes rather than features: loyalty is *"designed so the reward economics still work at month twelve"*, and gamification gives people *"a reason to come back before the reward does."* Small tags on each card (*On-pack codes*, *Instant win*, *Fulfilment*) give a quick read of the mechanics inside.

**Mechanics, as the vocabulary of a programme.** The Solutions page groups everything under three programmes, each tied to a business outcome:

- **Consumer & Trade Promotions** — on-pack QR with real-time validation, scan-to-play, receipt verification, spin-to-win, scratch cards, instant cashback, sampling and UGC contests
- **Brand Activations** — interactive microsites, quizzes and personality journeys, referral and share-to-unlock mechanics, festival IPs, WhatsApp-native journeys
- **Loyalty & Repeat Purchase** — earn-burn and tier-based loyalty, partner rewards, AI segmentation, win-back and reactivation journeys

Rather than listing these as bullet points on the homepage, a horizontally scrolling capabilities section, *"Everything a campaign needs, already built"*, shows them as campaigns people have played: Holi Heist 2 for Bigbasket, Scream to Jump for Pulse, the Love Language Quiz, TCL's AR Cricket. Each mechanic is seen working before it's named.

The method gets the same treatment: *Observe → Design → Deploy → Optimise* sits on a vertical red timeline that fills as you scroll, so the process reads as a story rather than a slide.

> A mechanic is easier to understand once you've watched someone play it.

## 09 — Industries

The industries section moves the conversation from *"here are our capabilities"* to *"here's how they apply to your category."* Each industry is stated as the specific problem it brings:

| Industry | The engagement problem |
| --- | --- |
| FMCG | On-pack promotions at national scale, reconciled to the last code |
| Retail | Store-level loyalty that works for staff, not just head office |
| Alcobev | Engagement inside strict compliance and age-gating rules |
| Pharma | Chemist and prescriber programmes with full auditability |
| Banking | Card-linked offers and reward economies that hold at volume |

On desktop, industries sit side by side as tall image columns that open on hover, each with a human scene from that category: a store shelf, a shopper's phone, a bar pour, a pharmacy counter, a card payment. On mobile they become a stack of full-width image cards. The line under each one is written the way someone in that industry would describe their own problem.

> The same platform, told five different ways, because a pharma brand and a beverage brand never ask the same first question.

## 10 — Case Studies & Campaign Storytelling

Campaigns are Octech's strongest proof, so the case studies needed to feel like the campaigns themselves rather than like documents about them.

**A consistent story structure.** Every case study follows the same arc:

1. **Objective** — what the brand wanted to achieve
2. **Journey & Mechanic** — the idea and the consumer's path through it, step by step
3. **Technology Delivered** — what was built
4. **Key Features** — what made the experience work

That consistency lets visitors compare campaigns, and makes the technology behind each one visible. A voice-controlled game turns out to be a voice and decibel engine, a scoring system, a score-card generator and a leaderboard.

**Campaign-specific presentation.** Each case study opens on a dark navy header with its category, then a large visual in the campaign's own world: the jungle runner of *Scream for Pulse*, the 70s Bollywood heist of *Holi Heist 2*, the stadium of *Ultra Vivid Cricket*. The journeys are written as the player lives them. In *Scream for Pulse*, you register, allow the microphone, shout the brand name to jump higher, collect candy for points, then share a personalised score card to climb the leaderboard.

**Filtering by mechanic.** The case-study index filters by *Gamification, Promotions, Loyalty* and *AI*, so a visitor interested in one kind of engagement finds the relevant proof quickly.

**Results.** The case studies currently focus on objective, mechanic and build rather than published performance figures. That's a gap I'd like to fill as verified results become available.

> Every case study is told as the consumer lives it: scan, play, score, share, reward.

## 11 — Motion & Interaction

Motion on Octech has a job: to make technology that's normally invisible feel **alive and experiential**. Here's where it does that work:

- **3D smartphone.** The hero's anchor, rendered in real time, showing real campaign screens rather than an abstract device.
- **Typewriter transitions.** The "We make…" line types, holds and erases, so the company's range unfolds one idea at a time.
- **Background transitions.** Each campaign brings its own environment, so every state feels like stepping into a different experience.
- **Custom cursor.** A cursor designed for the site replaces the system one, making browsing feel closer to interacting with a product.
- **Hover interactions.** Industry columns open on hover to reveal their story, keeping dense content one gesture away.
- **Horizontal capability scrolling.** A pinned section turns vertical scrolling into a sideways journey through campaigns, with a "Scroll →" cue.
- **Feathered navigation.** The floating navigation pill sits over a blurred backdrop that fades out downward. Content softly dissolves beneath it instead of meeting a hard bar, so the nav stays readable over both the dark hero and the light content.
- **Product interactions.** Product tabs switch the platform preview in place, and the method timeline fills as you scroll through it.
- **Micro-interactions.** Accordion FAQs, the floating "Chat with us" button, the persistent red call button, and smooth scrolling throughout.

> Motion here is explanation, not decoration. Every moving thing is showing how something works.

## 12 — AI & Visual Storytelling

AI is one of the hardest things to show. Stock imagery defaults to glowing brains and robots, which say nothing about what a product actually does. The approach here was to **show AI through what it produces and what it changes for a person**:

- **Product interfaces.** AnalyticsGenie and the intelligence layer appear as dashboards and plain-language insight, framed by the question a brand manager actually has: *"Your campaign is live. Do you actually know what's happening inside it?"*
- **Consumer experiences.** AI Experience Studio and the Personal Creative Generator are shown through their outputs: selfie-to-avatar transformations and branded personas, generated in about 1.8 seconds.
- **Personalisation.** The intelligence layer is described in terms of decisions, like the next best reward and the right moment, rather than models.
- **Creative generation.** GenStudio AI leads with a strip of real consumer creations: AI selfies, personalised anthems, custom pack designs, squad reels.
- **Analytics.** Four named measurement layers (participation, redemption, engagement quality and business outcomes) replace a generic "dashboard" promise.
- **Intelligent interactions.** Agentic integrations are framed as *"assistants, not dashboards"*: teams query and act on live data instead of waiting for a report.

> If AI is doing something useful, you can show the thing it did.

## 13 — Responsive Experience

The system was designed to hold together from a large desktop down to a phone, which matters because most of Octech's campaigns are played on phones.

- **Hero.** The headline stacks above the phone, the 3D phone becomes the centrepiece, and previous and next controls let visitors step through campaigns themselves.
- **Navigation.** The floating pill becomes a compact logo-and-menu bar with a red circular menu button. The menu opens as a rounded card with every section and a single red "Talk to us" action.
- **Cards.** Service cards stack with the image first, then title, promise and mechanic tags. Rounded containers keep each one self-contained.
- **Product experiences.** The product tabs become a row you swipe sideways, so moving between products still works one-handed.
- **Campaign visuals.** Campaign imagery keeps its full colour and scale, so the work stays the focus even on small screens.
- **Horizontal sections.** Sideways-scrolling sections and hover interactions are reworked for touch. Hover-to-open industries become stacked image cards, for example, and the method timeline still fills as you scroll.

> On a phone, the site should feel closer to the campaigns themselves, because that's where people play them.

## 14 — Outcome

These are **design outcomes**, evident in the finished site, not measured business results.

- **A clearer picture of Octech's ecosystem.** Programmes, platforms, intelligence, industries and proof each have a defined place, and the pages connect to each other.
- **Stronger product storytelling.** Every product answers the same two questions, *what's the problem* and *how does it work*, over real interfaces.
- **A more cohesive visual language.** One typeface, one brand red, a navy and neutral base, and a consistent label system give 50+ pages a single voice.
- **Better distinction between capabilities, products and industries.** Visitors can tell what Octech *does*, what it's *built on*, and *where it applies*.
- **More engaging interaction design.** The hero, horizontal capabilities and hover-driven industries let visitors experience the work, not just read about it.
- **A stronger technology-led brand presence.** Security, compliance, integrations and method sit alongside the playful work as first-class content.

> The result is a site that behaves like the product it describes: interactive, structured and built to keep people engaged.
